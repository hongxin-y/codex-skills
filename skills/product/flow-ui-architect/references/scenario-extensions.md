# Conditional interaction scenarios

Use the applicable branch only when its trigger is present. Record relevant decisions within the existing task, interaction, continuity and review steps. No branch requires an extra gate or new product module.

## Collaboration and delegated work

**Trigger:** work actually changes hands or several people share responsibility.

Distinguish creator, current assignee, observer and authorized actor where their differences matter. A role match alone does not establish personal responsibility. Separate work needing the user's action from waiting and monitoring.

After handoff, explain the destination, scope and outcome. A notification records an event; a work item represents unfinished responsibility. Reading a notification does not finish work, and saving a draft does not implicitly hand it off.

Personal completion, group completion and an accepted result can differ. Use the actual product rules. Do not add reviewer roles, assignment pages or approvals to a single-user product without a user need.

## Editing and creation

**Trigger:** users create, revise or annotate content.

Support the appropriate balance of direct editing, reading and comparison. High-frequency structured editing may need inline input; long-form creation may need a focused editor; occasional changes may start from readable content with an Edit action.

Keep persistence feedback accurate, preserve work on failure and support relevant undo or history. A changed marker should reflect the current difference from its stated baseline; returning to the baseline should remove it when that is what the marker means.

Distinguish original evidence or another person's comments from the current user's edits. Introduce versions, publication or audit history only when the product needs them.

## Long-running execution

**Trigger:** useful work continues beyond an immediate interaction.

Provide a stable place to understand activity and find results. Show known progress, partial output and actionable failure without forcing repeated refresh or blocking all other work.

Define start, retry, stop and cancellation according to real capabilities. Explain what stopping preserves, and avoid starting paid or consequential execution merely because its configuration page opened. Do not require a job-monitoring dashboard for an ordinary fast action.

## Bulk and large-collection work

**Trigger:** users process many objects or act on a selection.

Distinguish current item, current page, selected items and all matching items. Explain eligibility and skipped items. Preserve selection or make invalidation explicit when filters, permissions or underlying data change.

For partial completion, retain successful work and identify what remains. Choose retry behavior that avoids unintended repeated effects. Use search, grouping, pagination or bounded loading as appropriate; a large collection does not imply every product needs batch actions.

## Consequential transactions or commitments

**Trigger:** an action has meaningful financial, external, collaborative or difficult-to-reverse effects.

Expose the relevant scope, terms and consequence before commitment. Use confirmation proportional to the actual consequence; reversible routine actions may be better served by clear feedback and undo.

Do not interpret browsing, silence or saving as consent or approval when an explicit decision is required. Cancellation, withdrawal and refund can have different rules; label the actual capability. Derive permissions and recovery from known requirements rather than inventing an approval process.

## No scenario branch applies

A reading page, portfolio, simple comparison page or exploratory gallery may need none of these branches. Evaluate comprehension, orientation, decision support or discovery instead of adding work queues, completion badges and handoffs. Mixed products can use different branches on different surfaces.
