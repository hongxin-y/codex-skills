# Imagery, iconography and motion

## Imagery

Choose images, illustrations, textures or no imagery based on a real role:

- Orientation: helps users understand where they are.
- Education: explains a new or complex concept.
- Emotion: supports an intentional brand or onboarding moment.
- Evidence: communicates the object or result itself.
- Media navigation: helps users scan content.

Do not add an illustration to fill an empty state or hero when it does not improve orientation, education, emotion or brand recognition. Record source, licensing and loading behavior when implementing real assets.

## Icon grammar

Keep one coherent icon family unless there is a documented reason to combine families. Record stroke/fill behavior, viewBox proportions, optical weight, size roles, alignment and metaphor conventions. Icon-only controls need accessible names and should not carry essential meaning without text or another cue.

## Motion grammar

Use motion for:

- Spatial continuity when content moves between contexts.
- State feedback for save, loading, success or error.
- Hierarchy when a new decision or focus needs attention.
- Progressive disclosure when it clarifies what changed.

Avoid ambient motion that competes with a high-frequency task. Record the trigger, purpose, duration or easing when known, and reduced-motion behavior. Motion does not compensate for weak hierarchy.
