# Art direction comparison: consumer mobile discovery

## Context

A mobile app helps a user decide what to eat tonight and move to a real order. The primary task is a quick decision, not a preference-management dashboard.

## Visual Context Profile

- Product mode: Persuade plus Experience
- Audience: broad consumer audience with short attention and varied accessibility needs
- Session: occasional short decision
- Density: sparse at entry, richer after a recommendation
- Trust and risk: low to moderate; price, availability and delivery claims must be real
- Brand: warm and confident
- Content: food imagery, short copy, selection controls and recommendation evidence
- Device: touch-first mobile
- Differentiation: warm recommendation experience without generic orange gradients

## Candidate directions

### A — Warm editorial

Large food photography, readable editorial type, low-chroma warm neutral and one restrained action accent.

### B — Playful expressive

Characterful type, selective high-chroma accents, motion and illustration for exploration.

### C — Neutral product utility

Cool neutral, compact controls and low decoration for repeat ordering.

## Selection

Choose A because the task benefits from appetite and confidence while still requiring readable choice and evidence. Use expressive elements only when they clarify recommendation or brand. Reject B if motion or color competes with the decision. Reject C because it under-expresses the food and discovery context.

## Visual Grammar Contract

- Type: friendly display role plus highly readable UI sans.
- Grid: single-column touch-first flow with clear recommendation focus.
- Color: warm neutral foundation, restrained action accent, independent status roles.
- Shape: comfortable controls; capsules only for bounded filters and selected options.
- Surface: flat or lightly layered; no glass-heavy content cards.
- Imagery: real food or purposeful illustration, never decorative filler.
- Motion: quick recommendation and selection feedback with reduced-motion fallback.

## Stress cases

Review long restaurant names, missing imagery, unavailable items, price uncertainty, error recovery, large text, screen readers and narrow widths.
