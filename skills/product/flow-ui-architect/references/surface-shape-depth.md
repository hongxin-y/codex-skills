# Surface, shape and depth

## Surface model

Choose a surface model for the product and explain what it communicates:

- Flat: grouping through spacing, alignment and restrained borders.
- Border-led: clear structure and scanning for operations and data.
- Layered: distinct navigation, content and contextual layers.
- Elevated: limited depth for overlays, draggable objects or important separation.
- Translucent: controlled material for navigation/control layers or spatial media contexts.

Do not apply a material effect to every container. Content, tables and forms usually need stable backgrounds and clear contrast. Translucency must preserve text, focus and status readability.

## Shape grammar

Define a radius family and where each shape is allowed. Capsules are useful for tags, filters, search chips or deliberately expressive controls; they are not a default shape for every button, tab and card. A restrained direction should not mix random radii. A playful direction can use expressive shapes only when they reinforce brand and hierarchy.

## Borders and elevation

Use borders, elevation and background changes to clarify relationships, not to decorate empty space. Decide:

- Which surfaces are separated by spacing alone.
- Which need a border for scanning or focus.
- Which overlays need elevation.
- Which shadows are functional and which are ambient decoration.
- How hover, pressed, selected, disabled and focus states change depth.

## Cross-page contract

Keep surface, radius, border and depth semantics consistent across pages. A page may use a different density or composition for a different job, but should not invent a new surface language without recording why.
